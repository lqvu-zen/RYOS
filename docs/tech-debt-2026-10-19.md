# RYOS Technical Debt Review — 2026-10-19

_Snapshot at `f3630e0` (version 2.3.1, 2.4 step 35) · 23,695 lines across 78 modules in `ryos/` (`ryos/qtui/`: 24 files, 9,864 lines) · 1,577 unit tests in 9 files + 57 Qt smoke checks (`tests/qt_smoke.py`, 6,452 lines) · CI: `test`, `qt-smoke`, `typecheck`, `harness`._
_Supersedes [`tech-debt-2026-09-21.md`](tech-debt-2026-09-21.md), which describes the Tk app at 1.11.2. Priority = (Impact + Risk) × (6 − Effort), each rated 1–5. Measured with `ast` and `wc -l` on the tree above; line numbers are at `f3630e0`._

## Summary

Since the last review the package went from 11,628 lines in 30 modules to 23,695 in 78, and the toolkit changed under it: Tk (`app.py`, `dialogs.py`) is gone and the window is `ryos/qtui/`. The pattern the last review warned about repeated itself, in a new place. **Logic landed in pure, tested modules; the window class absorbed everything that touches a widget.** `ryos/qtui/shell.py` is 3,475 lines (3,136 at `2088a68`, the start of 2.4 work; +11%), `MainWindow` is 198 methods in 3,121 lines, and the self-update banner alone (steps 4–10) added about 390 of them. `RYOSApp` was the old god class; `MainWindow` is its successor, 10% bigger by methods.

What got better:

- **`mypy` covers 61% of lines** (14,515 of 23,695, 54 files), up from 49%. The three modules added for 2.4 part A (`swap`, `selfupdate`, `updates`) are all in scope.
- **The Tk-era blind spots are gone.** `dialogs.py` is 202 lines, not 1,864. The pystray/pillow question (old item 4) is moot: the tray is Qt.
- **Exception discipline held.** 21 broad `except Exception` in `ryos/`, one `type: ignore`, no `TODO`/`FIXME`/`XXX`/`HACK` markers. CI now rejects new comment lines under `ryos/` (`tools/harness_checks.py`).
- **The net is much bigger.** 652 → 1,577 unit tests, 16 → 57 GUI checks, plus `session_smoke`, `real_data_smoke`, `release_check` over four zips, fixture upgrades for every past schema, and the behaviour map (`docs/BEHAVIOUR.md`).

What did not move: `handle_step_done` (old item 3) went from 156 to **190 lines**, and the god-class problem is the same size in a new file. Steps 16–28 add migrations and accessors (16, 20, 23, 25, 27), new rules modules and a lot of window surface (14, 15, 18, 19, 22, 28). They all go through the places below.

## Findings

### 1. `handle_step_done` is 190 lines — Priority 36

`ryos/job_controller.py:572`. In the last review it was 156 lines and "a third of the module"; it is now 190 of 761. Nothing split it, and steps 23 (pipeline parameters) and 24 (a value passed between steps) both change what a finished step does: the first at launch, the second at completion, where "the last one to finish wins" for parallel steps (PL-03, PL-09). The module is toolkit-free and in `mypy` scope, so there is no excuse for size.

**Impact 4, Risk 5, Effort 2.** The order-of-operations bugs found last time (settle-then-mark, lazy head-only skipping) lived exactly here, and step 24 adds a new ordering rule to the same scope. Splitting it into named sub-steps (retry, group settle, failure policy, record a value, advance) under the existing `TestJobController` net is a refactor with a safety net already in place.

**Extraction candidate for steps 23, 24:** do this before 23 starts; the step then adds a named sub-step instead of another branch.

### 2. Menu dispatch in `shell.py` — Priority 28

`card_menu_items` (2680), `_show_card_menu`, `on_card_menu` (2714), `_show_group_menu` (2856), `on_group_menu` (2865, 74 lines) and the dialogs they ask through (`_ask_yes_no`, `_ask_text`, `_ask_import_mode`): about 360 lines. The menu contents are already data in `ryos/cardmenu.py` (469 lines, pure, pinned by `TestCardMenu`). What stays in the window is the `key → action` dispatch, which has to know everything the window can do.

Steps 14 and 15 (backlog), 26 (secrets: the delete prompt, clone and copy), and 28 (a trigger entry, or the schedule dialog, and RC-20's pinned order) all add keys and branches here. Each is a new `elif` in a 74-line method on a 3,475-line class.

**Impact 4, Risk 4, Effort 3.**

**Extraction candidate for steps 14, 15, 26, 28:** `qtui/menuactions.py`, holding a table `key → handler` and the group and card actions, with the window passed in as a small interface (as `jobhost` is passed to `headless`). The dispatch then gets a unit test without a display, and step 28 adds one table row.

### 3. `qtui` is invisible to `mypy` — Priority 24

9,864 lines in 24 files, 42% of the package; only `qtui/__init__.py` and `qtui/stylesheet.py` are in `[tool.mypy] files`, because the typecheck job does not install PySide6. This is the same blind spot as old item 1, now at the size of the whole window. It has real consequences: step 10's banner and `MainThreadInvoker` hand-offs, step 22's live records and step 27's timer all cross the thread boundary through `qtui`, and nothing but `qt_smoke` looks at them.

**Impact 3, Risk 3, Effort 2.** Step 36 already owns this. The advice here is to start with the files the 16–28 steps change (`shell.py`, `cards.py`, `detail.py`, `smalldialogs.py`, `scriptdialog.py`, `activity.py`, `running.py`, `pipeline.py`, `tray.py`), install `PySide6` in the job, and add the files in that order. `shell.py` will not go clean in one step; the extraction in items 2, 4 and 6 is what makes it cheap.

### 4. Jobs, status and Activity block in `shell.py` — Priority 24

`attach_jobs` (3123), `_on_job_started`, `_refresh_card_statuses`, `_live_statuses`, `_stop_item`, `sync_layout` (3237), `refresh_activity` (3326), `_open_from_activity`, plus the output-tab methods (`add_output_tab`, `_close_output_tab`, `append_output`) around 3045–3130: about 350 lines that decide what a running job looks like in every layout. `qtui/running.py` (182) and `qtui/activity.py` (142) hold the widgets; the rules for what to show are in `ryos/activity.py` and `ryos/detail.py`, but the glue is still in the window.

Steps 18 (time left in the Running list, status bar, tray), 19, 22 (CLI and agent runs listed in the Running list while they run) and 28 (the Activity bar says what is watched) all change this glue, and the plan says each must be drawn in normal, compact and maximised (surface table, steps 14 and 15).

**Impact 4, Risk 4, Effort 3.**

**Extraction candidate for steps 18, 22, 28:** a `qtui/runstate.py` that owns "what is running and how it reads", fed by `JobRegistry` and, for step 22, by the live records of runs from other processes. The designer for step 22 should decide that boundary first, since it has to change this block either way.

### 5. `ScriptDB` — Priority 24

`ryos/db.py` is 1,651 lines; `ScriptDB` is 73 methods in 1,306. `import_from_file` is 145 lines, `export_to_file` 95, `clone_group` 84. Steps 16, 20, 23, 25 and 27 each add a migration and an accessor; 26 adds export and import fields (IE-02, IE-03). The rules that protect this file are good: `_ensure_baseline` is frozen, `_MIGRATIONS` re-check `PRAGMA table_info`, `TestRowWidthsArePinned` pins accessor widths, `TestFixtureUpgrades` runs every past release's database. The weakness is size: five steps edit one file and one 145-line import method, and `configio.py` (wording only) is the wrong place for the logic.

**Impact 4, Risk 4, Effort 3.**

**Extraction candidate for steps 26 (secrets in export) and 27 (triggers cascade):** move export and import (the two long methods) to a module that takes a `ScriptDB`, so each of these steps adds a section to a small function. Do not touch `_ensure_baseline` or the row widths.

### 6. Self-update banner block in `shell.py` — Priority 20

`check_for_updates` (1769) to `show_start_notes` (2105): `_start_update_download`, `_update_downloaded`, `_offer_restart`, `show_update_state`, `_build_update_banner`, `_decide`, `restart_to_update`, `roll_back_update`, `_wait_for_runs`, `_prepare_in_background`, `_launch_helper`, `_refresh_rollback_action`: about 390 lines, all written in steps 4–10. The decisions are in `selfupdate.py` (pure, 476 lines, tested). The remaining code is state handling for six banner states and four hand-offs between threads.

No step in 16–28 changes it, so the risk is not growth but that it has never run for real (STATUS: "Self-update is untested for real"). Steps 11 and 12 are the first test. If they find fixes, they land in this block.

**Impact 3, Risk 3, Effort 2.** Not urgent. Extract `qtui/updatebanner.py` when step 12 finds something to fix in it, not before.

### 7. `tests/qt_smoke.py` — Priority 20

57 `check_*` functions in one 6,452-line script that runs in one process, in order, ending with a summary. It is the only test for the whole window, and the CI job that runs it (`qt-smoke (ubuntu, offscreen)`) exits 139 about half the time after the last check (step 37, not diagnosed here). Even once that is fixed, a single script means one failing or crashing check hides all those after it, and each new feature adds a check to the same file (steps 14–28 name `qt_smoke` cases in their "Done when" lists).

**Impact 3, Risk 4, Effort 3.** Do after step 37 shows whether the crash lives in the script's teardown. If it does, a runner that takes `--only` and one process per area (cards, dialogs, update, tray) fixes it and the size problem together.

### 8. `cli.py` — Priority 16

811 lines, no class; `_parser` is 111 lines, `main` 94, `_update` 95, `_apply_update` 80. Steps 16/17 (`--fail-on`), 19 (`history --stats`), 23 (`pipeline --param`) and 26 (`secret set|remove|list`) each add a subcommand and its argparse wiring to `_parser`. The module is in `mypy` scope and `tests/test_cli.py` (1,283 lines) runs real tiny scripts, so the net is good.

**Impact 3, Risk 2, Effort 3.** Split the update commands (`_update`, `_apply_update`, about 175 lines, which belong to `selfupdate`'s world) out of `cli.py` when the next subcommand is added, so `_parser` stays readable.

### 9. Long constructors — Priority 15

`MainWindow.__init__` 179 lines, `DetailPane.__init__` 173, `ScriptCard.__init__` 109, `ScheduleDialog.__init__` 102. In the Tk review this was the `_build_*` pattern; the sub-builder fix worked (`_refresh_cards` 219 → 46). The Qt constructors have the same shape and have grown with every surface (normal, compact, maximised). Steps 14, 15 (every row surface), 18, 19 (stats in the pane) and 28 (the TRIGGER badge) touch the card and pane constructors.

**Impact 2, Risk 3, Effort 2.** Do it when a step touches the constructor, by moving the part it changes into a named `_build_*`.

### 10. `shell.py` as a whole — Priority 10 (root cause)

3,475 lines, 225 functions, `MainWindow` 198 methods. Its responsibilities, by line range: output pane and tabs (65–354, 669–905, 3045–3130), menu bar and cards (910–1475), geometry and placement (1552–1760), self-update (1769–2160), tray and single-instance (2162–2300), quit (2287–2320), drop and import/export (2321–2512), select mode (2513–2680), menus (2680–3040), jobs, status and Activity (3123–3350), group picker and detail (3354–3475). Items 2, 4, 6 and 9 are slices of it. It scores low only because a full split is high-effort; as in the last review, that is a statement about sequencing.

**Impact 4, Risk 4, Effort 5.** Plan step 36 already says no big refactor in 2.4 and sets the rule: a step that changes `shell.py` moves the part it touches into its own module. Items 2 and 4 are the two slices where that rule will apply to the most steps.

### 11. `stylesheet()` — Priority 8

`ryos/qtui/stylesheet.py:196`, one function of 638 lines that builds the whole application stylesheet from a palette. It is `mypy`-checked and `TestQtStylesheet` covers legibility in every theme, so the risk is low; step 17 (error and warning highlights in every shipped theme) will add to it.

**Impact 2, Risk 2, Effort 2.** Leave until step 17; split by widget family then.

## What's healthy (don't "fix")

- **Toolkit-free core.** The one-way import rule holds: `ryos/*.py` never import `qtui`; 54 of 78 files are type-checked, and the core is unit-tested without a display.
- **Pure modules for new logic.** `swap`, `selfupdate`, `updates`, `signature`, `ed25519`, `agenttools`, `activity`, `detail`, `cardmenu`, `selection`, `sections`, `traypolicy` and more are small, tested, and take their effects as arguments.
- **Schema discipline.** Frozen baseline, add-only migrations, pinned accessor widths, a fixture database per past release.
- **Release gates.** `release_check` over four zips, `launch_smoke`, `session_smoke`, `real_data_smoke`, signed updates, and the escape rule that adds a check for each bug found after a release.
- **Harness.** Hooks that refuse writes into the real data folder, CI checks for `__version__` and new comments, `ask`/`deny` rules (step 31).
- **Exception discipline and markers.** 21 broad guards, 1 `type: ignore`, 0 TODO markers.
- **Docs that are checked.** `docs/BEHAVIOUR.md` maps behaviours to tests; `TestDocsMatchTheCode` catches drift.

## Top three worth doing next

1. **Split `handle_step_done` before step 23.** Highest priority and lowest effort (36), and it is the one place where order-of-operations bugs have already occurred.
2. **Extract menu dispatch from `shell.py` at step 14,** so that steps 15, 26 and 28 add table rows instead of branches (item 2), and put `shell.py` and the files named in item 3 into `mypy` at step 36.
3. **Settle the jobs/Activity boundary at the step 22 design,** before 18 and 22 both grow the same block (item 4); and look at item 5's import/export split at step 26.

Not in the top three, with reasons: item 7 waits for step 37's diagnosis; items 6 and 8 wait for something to touch them.
